The first deadline-role protected screen found one tier-2 gain and one tier-3
loss. A same-binary switch proved both mobile-hub effects can be causal, while
the rare-brand role mask also exposes a genuine cross-assignment ranking effect.

This frozen screen measures the candidate against an identical-layout control.
It is not a new candidate and must not be used to tune mobile ordering, limits,
or dispatch. The acceptance question is whether gains are diverse and material
while losses remain rare, small, and non-systematic across role mode, map scale,
and fuel. Any tier-1 loss, systematic tier-2 loss, invalid/emergency, or large
tail downside rejects candidate 142.

## Result

The first preregistered 12-case deadline-role block was sufficient to reject
142, so the remaining blocks were not opened. Same-binary candidate versus
control was `2/8/2`, invalid/emergency zero. Seed 316004 retained role mask 8
but fell from `6/24/31` to `6/23/28`; seed 316005 changed mask 4 to 8 and fell
from `6/24/30` to `6/22/29`. Gains were seed 316000 tier-3 `+1` and seed
316011 tier-2/tier-3 `6/22/25 -> 6/24/30`. Two tier-2 losses in twelve cases,
including one under an unchanged role assignment, violate the frozen gate.

The causal boundary is future-state coupling: an immediate-day mobile result
can lexicographically beat the static incumbent yet consume fuel or change own
traffic so a later day loses tier 2. This mechanism is globally safe without a
future proof only on the public terminal day, where no suffix remains.
